Research on: Pre-Acquisition Operational & Regulatory Due Diligence for Cold-Chain Logistics

Hackathon: Enterprise Risk & Supply Chain Intelligence Challenge

Objectives:
Your challenge is to conduct an unfiltered due diligence investigation and produce a risk dossier that helps corporate M&A leadership evaluate a target cold-chain pharmaceutical logistics provider before signing an exclusivity agreement. You may gather evidence from public corporate filings, regulatory enforcement databases, court dockets, and industry trade reports. Any verifiable public record can be used, as long as it tests the operational reliability and compliance posture of the target company over the past 36 months—such as FDA inspection classifications, temperature-excursion recalls, or labor litigation filings.

You can use public regulatory repositories (such as the FDA Data Dashboard, EMA EudraGMDP, OSHA inspection logs, and federal/state court records) to verify whether the target company's operational track record matches its public marketing claims. What specific quality assurances does the company advertise on its website and investor decks? Why might a facility receive an FDA Form 483 observation or warning letter? How do historical cold-chain failures or labor disputes impact pharmaceutical client retention? When does an absence of public records indicate clean compliance versus sealed litigation or private-company reporting exemptions? What contractual warranties should leadership demand based on uncovered risks? These are questions worth considering as you develop your solution.

Evidence verification and epistemic clarity play an important role in executive risk reporting. You may consider taking advantage of side-by-side claim-versus-evidence comparisons and explicit confidence tags to present the information. For example, your dossier could place the company's published on-time temperature compliance rate directly beside verified regulatory inspection findings and litigation summaries.

Potential Considerations:
You may (but are not required to) consider the following:

Your investigation could evaluate risk across distinct operational pillars, such as regulatory compliance, fleet/warehouse infrastructure, financial solvency, and labor stability.

Your dossier could explicitly document "dead ends"—queries where records are behind paywalls, sealed by court order, or unavailable for private entities—without speculating on missing facts.

Your output could include the following features:

A discrepancy ledger contrasting public PR statements against verified adverse records

Severity ratings (Low, Medium, Critical) assigned to each uncovered compliance or legal finding

An audit trail of search queries and databases checked so human analysts can verify negative findings

Do not assume that executives have time to read raw legal dockets or technical inspection reports. Your dossier should translate regulatory jargon into clear financial and operational deal-breakers.
