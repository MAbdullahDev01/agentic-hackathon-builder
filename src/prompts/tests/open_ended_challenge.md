Research on: Second-Order Economic and Geopolitical Effects of Commercial Deep-Sea Mining

Hackathon: Global Ocean Governance & Critical Minerals Challenge

Objectives:
Your challenge is to produce a comprehensive policy briefing and interactive risk assessment tool that helps policymakers evaluate the second-order economic, environmental, and geopolitical consequences of commercial deep-sea mining in the Clarion-Clipperton Zone over the next 15 years. You may consider gathering data from primary academic studies, International Seabed Authority (ISA) filings, national regulatory frameworks, and commodity market reports. Any kind of quantitative metric can be used, as long as it represents a verifiable economic or environmental indicator, such as cobalt export dependency ratios or benthic biodiversity impact scores.

You can use International Seabed Authority (ISA) environmental management plans, alongside publicly available geological and trade databases (such as USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries and UN Comtrade), to understand how seabed mineral extraction disrupts land-based supply chains. Which terrestrial mining economies face the sharpest revenue contraction? Why is governance fragmenting between multilateral ISA rules and parallel national licensing pathways? How do deep-sea extraction costs compare against terrestrial refining? When might mineral price shocks warrant sovereign wealth interventions or trade protections? What policy mechanisms can be implemented to prevent ecological and economic destabilization? These are questions worth considering as you develop your solution.

Synthesis and structure play an important role in presenting complex geopolitical trade-offs clearly. You may consider taking advantage of comparative frameworks and structured citations to present the information. For example, your briefing could map vulnerable land-based mining nations alongside their primary mineral exports, projected price elasticity, and recommended fiscal buffers.

Potential Considerations:
You may (but are not required to) consider the following:

Your analysis could evaluate outcomes under different regulatory scenarios, such as full adoption of the ISA Mining Code, widespread national moratoria, or unilateral national licensing regimes.

Your briefing could identify specific trigger thresholds—such as sustained drops in global nickel or cobalt prices—that require immediate policy action.

Your output could include the following features:

A risk-scoring matrix that ranks land-based mining economies by their vulnerability to seabed mineral supply shocks

Comparative timelines showing how regulatory divergence between international and national frameworks unfolds over 5-, 10-, and 15-year horizons

Clear source attribution that separates verified empirical data from economic projections

Do not assume that decision-makers are familiar with marine geology or international maritime law. Your briefing should provide concise explanations covering key mechanisms, regulatory bodies, and systemic risks.
